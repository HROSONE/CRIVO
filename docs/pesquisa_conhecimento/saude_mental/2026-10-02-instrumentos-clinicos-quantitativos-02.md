# Instrumentos clínicos quantitativos — lote 2

**Data:** 2026-10-02
**Regra:** measure ≠ diagnosis; severity ≠ etiology; reliability ≠ validity; responsiveness ≠ clinical utility.

## INS-004 — International Trauma Questionnaire (ITQ)
- alinhado aos constructs ICD-11 PTSD/CPTSD;
- development/finalization: trauma-exposed community N=1.051 + clinical N=247 UK;
- IRT used to select optimal symptom indicators;
- optimized 12-item structure supported by CFA;
- clinical validation anterior: N=171 British trauma-exposed clinical sample;
- two-factor second-order solution supported PTSD/CPTSD distinction;
- diagnostic rates ICD-11 PTSD were lower than DSM-5 PTSD in that sample.
- Portuguese/Angolan psychometric work: N=268 convenience sample.

**Limite:** ITQ operationalizes ICD-11 constructs; factor fit does not prove PTSD/CPTSD are distinct biological diseases.
**Uso:** assessment within broader clinical evaluation.

## INS-005 — PANSS
Original development/reliability:
- classic 1988 study N=82 acute/chronic schizophrenia;
- interrater reliabilities in .80s;
- criterion/construct validity evidence.

Historical replication:
- N=100 DSM-III-R schizophrenia;
- positive/negative scales good interrater reliability and concurrent validity;
- positive/negative factors insufficient to capture all schizophrenia symptoms.

Modern COSMIN systematic review/meta-analysis (2025):
- 119 publications;
- sufficient reliability, construct validity and responsiveness;
- significant shortcomings in content and structural validity;
- original three-factor model had poor structural validity and was rated not recommendable under COSMIN;
- negative subscale misses some NIMH consensus negative-symptom domains;
- administration ~30–50 min limits routine feasibility.

PANSS-6 COSMIN 2025:
- sufficient results for several measurement properties;
- 15–20 min;
- greater feasibility but intentionally incomplete symptom coverage.

**Crucial:** “most widely used” ≠ “psychometrically optimal”.

## INS-006 — MADRS
Classic validation:
- N=44 depressed inpatients;
- construct/concurrent validity and inter-rater reliability supported;
- sleep, appetite and suicidal-thought items correlated less strongly with remainder.

Brazilian sample:
- N=91 bipolar I or major depressive disorder patients in São Paulo;
- HAM-D and MADRS showed adequate reliability/validity for depressive symptoms;
- neither had sensitivity/specificity adequate for cutoff differentiating bipolar I from unipolar depression.

**Consequence:** severity of depression cannot diagnose unipolar vs bipolar etiology/course.

## INS-007 — HAM-D
- historically common clinician-rated depression severity scale;
- multiple versions/item sets complicate comparability;
- psychometric critiques include multidimensionality/item weighting and somatic content.
- Rasch re-analysis found full HAM-D17 and MADRS10 did not fit unidimensional Rasch model in that dataset; HAM-D6 performed better under those analyses.

**Rule:** always specify version (e.g., HAM-D17), not just “Hamilton”.

# Diagnostic-scale hierarchy
1. symptom presence;
2. symptom severity;
3. functional impact;
4. screening/classification;
5. diagnosis;
6. prognosis;
7. treatment prediction.

Evidence at level 2 cannot be silently promoted to levels 5–7.
